About

I am Professor of Philosophy at Lawrence University in Appleton, Wisconsin, where I direct the Program in Cognitive Science and co-direct the university’s AI Working Group.

My research concerns the normative structure of communication and cooperative relationships, pragmatics and speech acts, and mental state attribution. Much of my work sits at the intersection of philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, and cognitive science, and often proceeds experimentally. Recent projects take up the value of friendship, how we attribute mental states to others, and the question of whether large language models can have intentions.

Before coming to Lawrence in 2011, I was a Fellow in Cognitive Science at Yale University. I received my Ph.D. in Philosophy from the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill in 2010.

Research Areas

Primary topics: the normative structure of communication and cooperative relationships; pragmatics and speech acts; mental state attribution.

Specializations & competencies: philosophy of language, philosophy of mind, cognitive science, philosophy of psychology, philosophy of art, epistemology, philosophy of science.
